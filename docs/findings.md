# Cross-Venue Prediction Market Microstructure: Findings from NBA Finals 2026

*Aditya N. Bhosale — May 2026*

This work began as a search problem and ended as a structural one. The intent was to find prediction markets cross-listed on Kalshi and Polymarket with enough bilateral liquidity to support cross-venue analysis. The search returned exactly one category — 2026 NBA Finals team-win markets — across four candidate domains (sports, macro/Fed, politics, crypto). Three of those NBA Finals markets (OKC, CLE, NYK) had combined open interest near $110M at curation time, enough to make their microstructure worth examining in detail. The asymmetry of the discovery itself is the first finding: the cross-venue prediction-market universe is much narrower than the per-venue universes suggest, and that narrowness is structural rather than incidental. Kalshi lists CFTC-regulated event contracts that have no Polymarket counterpart; Polymarket lists cultural and tail-event markets that Kalshi avoids. Where the universes do overlap, they overlap thinly.

The headline empirical finding sits in the OKC market. Across four timestamped snapshots spanning roughly twenty-two hours, the paper mid-price discrepancy between Kalshi and Polymarket on the OKC YES contract decayed from 1.00¢ to 0.50¢ to 0.00¢, and then held at 0.00¢ across a confirming snapshot. No executable arbitrage ever fired — the paper edge sat below the fee threshold. Yet the two venues equilibrated to identical mids without any incentive that the standard arbitrage model can name. The most natural reading is that market makers operating on one venue are paying attention to the other, and absorbing small dislocations as a matter of pricing hygiene rather than profit capture. This is convergence by attention, not by mechanism, and it is exactly the kind of pattern that becomes invisible if you only look at executed trades.

No takeable cross-venue arb exists at any accessible fee tier on Kalshi/Polymarket. Under corrected fees (Kalshi parabolic 7¢×C×(1−C), Polymarket category-dependent 3-4%), 0 of 15 markets show takeable arb. Direction-enforced maker scenarios show 8 markets with provideable spread, but capturing it requires resting passive and waiting for incoming flow — an LP edge, not an arb edge. A counterfactual sweep finds that takeable arb would appear at ~0.30% taker / 0.20% maker rebate fees (8 markets, ~$73/snapshot), but neither venue offers this tier to any market participant as of 2026-05-28.



The Polymarket side of the dataset revealed three distinct quote-provision states that Kalshi does not appear to share. Active markets with substantial remaining uncertainty (the OKC contract, where the Thunder are mid-series in the Western Conference Finals) remain fully quoted on both sides at meaningful prices. Markets that have just experienced a major news-driven repricing event — the NYK contract, where the Knicks clinched their Finals appearance on 2026-05-26 — temporarily enter "lighthouse mode": the book remains structurally active with many price levels, but quotes have collapsed to the extreme tails (best_bid 0.001, best_ask 0.999) while market-makers re-evaluate championship probability against a TBD Finals opponent. Post-elimination outcomes (CLE YES, where the Cavaliers were eliminated by the Knicks in the ECF) are fully delisted: the CLOB returns "No orderbook exists" on a structurally valid token ID. Kalshi behaves differently — it maintains active books with multi-million-contract boundary stubs at $0.01 on both sides of news-repriced or post-elimination markets, rather than withdrawing quotes or delisting. The convention difference is itself a finding: two venues with overlapping inventory exhibit different defaults for how to handle markets in transition.

The three markets together exhibit three distinct microstructure regimes despite resolving on the same event series. OKC is the clean case: similar mids on both venues, similar spreads, depth on both sides, and the convergence behavior described above. NYK is the post-clinch repricing case: both venues entered temporary quote-collapse states on 2026-05-26 after the Knicks secured their Finals appearance — Polymarket quoting 0.001/0.999 with a structurally active book of 125+ bids and 90+ asks, Kalshi maintaining multi-million-contract bids at $0.01 on both YES and NO sides. The simple midpoint computation reports both venues at $0.50, which is degenerate by construction; the structural finding is that the two venues independently arrive at a similar quote-withdrawal response to a major news-driven repricing event with the championship odds still uncertain against a TBD Finals opponent. CLE is the post-elimination tail-probability case: Polymarket has fully delisted YES (Cavaliers eliminated by the Knicks in the ECF), while Kalshi maintains a one-sided book with no YES bids at any price and a deep NO bid stack pricing CLE at ~99.6% NO. The same event yields three different liquidity shapes, and the regime is determined by where each market sits in the resolution arc — pre-clinch active, post-clinch repriced, or post-elimination.

The framing that ties these findings together is that the cross-venue value sits in observability rather than arbitrage capture. Every executable arb computation in this study returned zero — paper discrepancies exist but do not cross the realistic fee threshold. The convergence pattern, the lifecycle differences between venues, and the regime differentiation are all real, but none of them produce a trade. They produce information about how the prediction-market structure actually behaves, and that information is what a real-time cross-venue terminal exists to surface. The arb is a red herring; the structure is the product.

**Live Theses:** Two directions survive Stage-1 closure. Both are structurally different from arb-taking and neither requires institutional fee access.
Thesis A — Liquidity provision (EXP-12). Under direction-enforced maker execution, 8 markets show flow-contingent provideable spread, anchored by concrete dollar magnitudes per contract: arod +0.138¢ (mixed mode, K taker / PM maker), co_aesp +0.670¢ once the Polymarket 25% maker rebate is active, pe_rpal +1.171¢, kr_oseh +1.190¢, la_kbas +1.700¢, co_aesp +2.670¢ (both-maker). These are LP edges — captured by resting passive and waiting to be lifted by incoming taker flow, not by sweeping contra-venue depth. The agent shape is market-making with inventory and fill risk, not arbitrage. Open empirical questions: fill realism (queue priority, adverse selection on lifts), inventory management across two venues with different settlement currencies (Kalshi USD vs Polymarket USDC-on-Polygon), and whether the PM maker rebate is reliably accruable in practice.
Thesis B — Cross-venue latency / lead-lag (EXP-4). Two independent observations point to Kalshi re-rating faster than Polymarket as new information arrives. First, the NYK market: post-ECF Game 6 (Knicks clinching, 2026-05-26), the bucket drifted mid_low → central → mid_low over ~36 hours, with the cross-venue mid-discrepancy reaching −0.95¢ Kalshi-richer at the D.2 snapshot, indicating Kalshi participants re-rated faster than Polymarket as Spurs/OKC information arrived. Second observation pending: the May 31 Colombia presidential first-round count (F.1 dense capture armed for the 22:00 UTC → 06:00 UTC window) provides a clean political-catalyst test of the same directional asymmetry on different markets, in a different geography. Edge in this thesis does not require fee-tier access — it requires being correctly positioned before the slower venue reprices, which is information advantage, not fee advantage. The infrastructure dependency is real: this thesis requires websocket clients on both venues (neither currently implemented), and the lead-lag measurement scaffold (scripts/window_event.py) is already built. F.2 (post-event analysis) is the first formal lead-lag measurement.
Closed: the original cross-venue arb-as-taking-the-cross thesis. EXP-1 as a cross-venue arbitrage simulator is deprioritized; if revived, it would be re-scoped as an LP-strategy simulator under Thesis A. EXP-10 (triangular/synthetic arb) inherits the same execution constraints and is deprioritized.
